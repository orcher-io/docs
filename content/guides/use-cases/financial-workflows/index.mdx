Payment processing is where durable execution matters most — a crashed server mid-charge can mean a customer charged twice, an order stuck in limbo, or revenue lost to unreconciled transactions. Orcher journals every task's result, so a completed charge, refund or inventory operation is never repeated when a run recovers; the tasks pass idempotency keys so a retry after a crash can't repeat them either.

### Charged once

The nightmare scenario is a crash in the gap between charging the card and recording that you did. With journaled tasks the gap doesn't exist for the workflow: once `charge_payment` completes, its result is in the journal, and no replay will ever run it again.

### Rollback is a flow, not a scramble

Multi-step money movement fails halfway: the charge succeeded, but inventory is gone or the shipping service is down. The saga pattern gives every step a compensating action, and the workflow runs them in reverse — automatically, with the same durability as the forward path.

### Waiting on the outside world

Payments rarely finish in one pass. A transaction may pause for a fraud analyst's verdict, a webhook from the payment provider, or a 3-day retry schedule — and a workflow waiting on an event or timer is fully suspended, holding no resources until the answer arrives. See [Fraud Detection](/guides/use-cases/financial-workflows/fraud-detection) for event gates with timeouts and [Subscription Billing](/guides/use-cases/financial-workflows/subscription-billing) for retry schedules that span days.

And because every step is journaled with its inputs, outputs, and timing, the journal is a record of what each payment did, produced by running it rather than by a separate logging system.
