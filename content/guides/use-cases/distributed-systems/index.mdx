Coordinating multiple microservices into a reliable end-to-end transaction is one of the hardest problems in distributed systems. A payment service might succeed while the inventory service fails, leaving your data inconsistent and your customers confused. Orcher solves this by letting you write distributed workflows as regular code — with every cross-service call journaled to PostgreSQL.

### The transaction state, made explicit

The classic distributed failure isn't that a service goes down — it's that afterwards *nobody knows where the transaction stands*. When every service call runs through a journaled task, that question always has an answer.

Either recovery path is durable: retries pick up exactly the calls that never completed, and rollback runs [saga compensations](/guides/use-cases/distributed-systems/saga-pattern) in reverse order — themselves journaled, so an interrupted rollback resumes instead of restarting.

### Compose along service boundaries

Child workflows let the orchestration mirror your architecture: one child per service, each owning its service's steps, retries, and failure handling — independently testable, reusable across parent workflows.

### Waiting on other systems

Distributed transactions rarely stay inside your infrastructure. A step may need a webhook from a third party, a human approval, or a callback that arrives hours later — [event gates](/guides/use-cases/distributed-systems/event-gates) pause the workflow at zero resource cost until the answer arrives or a timeout decides for it.
